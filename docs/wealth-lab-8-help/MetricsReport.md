## Metrics Report

This visualizer provides a detailed report of the trading system's performance statistics that describe the results of the Strategy. A list of performance metrics is presented in the first column on the left. The choice of metrics depends on installed ScoreCards: out of the box, Basic Scorecard contains various key performance metrics broken by categories like:

- Summary (key metrics like Profit, Exposure, Sharpe and Sortino ratios etc.)
- Positions, 
- Drawdown, 
- Profitable/Unprofitable Positions

More ScoreCards are available from [Extensions page](https://www.wealth-lab.com/Extension).

 
### Metrics
For [Multi-Currency backtests](BacktestPreferences), metrics are provided in the selected **Base Currency**. 

**Profit**  
The total dollar profit generated. This is the sum of the net profits, reduced by commissions and slippage, for each trade generated by the system.

**Net Profit %**  
Total Net Profit expressed as a percentage of Starting Capital.

**Profit per Bar**  
This Profit per Bar metric is independent of the Position Size used for the backtest. It's calculated by giving each trade a $5,000 fixed position size and dividing the sum of the trade profits by the sum of the number of bars held by the trades. In Futures Mode the trade size is $5,000 divided by the contract margin. 

Profit per Bar provides a fair comparison to the Benchmark's performance, and gives us an idea of the efficiency of the trading system. Even though a system doesn't beat the Benchmark's performance in terms of net profit, if it has a higher Profit per Bar we can say that it's *more efficient* than the buy-and-hold Benchmark strategy. We can exploit this efficiency by applying more aggressive Position sizing techniques in a Portfolio backtest to increase actual profit.

**APR** (a.k.a. Annualized Gain %, Annual Percentage Return)  
APR is the Compounded Annual Growth Rate, or CAGR.  CAGR provides a smoothed average rate of return as if the starting equity were compounded annually.

**Exposure (%)**  
The total market exposure of the trading strategy calculated on a bar by bar basis.  The Exposure metric measures the area of portfolio equity that was exposed to the market. For example, say you have $100,000 and buy $25,000 worth of stock at the beginning of a simulation.  If the prices of your purchases never change, you would have a flat equity line at $100K (assuming 0% interest on free cash) and an "exposed" line $25K. Notice that this forms a rectangle within a rectangle, and therefore exposure is 25%, which is the percentage of the equity curve exposed to the market. 

**Sharpe Ratio**  
The Sharpe Ratio is a way to measure the risk-adjusted return of an investment.  Its ratio measures how much of an investment's return can be attributed to chance. A Sharpe Ratio value of above 1.0 is considered good, while a value above 2.0 is typically considered outstanding.

The Sharpe Ratio calculation assumes a true to life rate of return based on the real historical values of 1-Year U.S. Treasury yield.

The Sharpe Ratio is calculated by obtaining the average of monthly percentage equity returns generated by the system, as well as the standard deviation of those returns. The risk-free rate of return is subtracted from the average monthly return and then annualized by multiplying by 12. This result is divided by the standard deviation of the returns multiplied by Sqrt(12).


**Sortino Ratio**  
Drawing from the concept of "bad" risk measured as the negative deviation of a portfolio's returns from the mean, the Sortino ratio differs from the Sharpe ratio in that it only considers the standard deviation of the downside risk.

Because of that, it is believed to have a better focus on portfolio's risk-adjusted performance by excluding positive volatility as a benefit.

**WL Score**  
The goal of the Wealth-Lab Score is to arrive at a single metric that encompasses profitability, exposure (efficiency) and risk.  Wealth-Lab Score is annualized, consequently simulation results that span time periods of different lengths may be compared.

It is calculated by first finding the Annualized Risk Adjusted Return, which is simply the Annual Percentage Return, or APR, divided by percent Exposure. For profitable systems, the RAR is adjusted by multiplying the sum of 1 plus the Max Drawdown Percent (a negative number). Consequently, profitable but risky systems, which typically have periods with large drawdowns, will have a lower score than an equally profitable system that assumes less risk.

For example, with an APR of 8.0, an Exposure of 25%, and a Max Drawdown of -15.0%, the Wealth-Lab Score is:

( 8.0 / 0.25 ) * ( 1 - 0.15 ) = 32 * 0.85 = 27.20

Non-profitable systems result in a negative Wealth-Lab Score.  The formula, which changes for negative RAR, more heavily penalizes non-profitable systems for their inability to overcome drawdown.  Consequently, if xRAR is less than 0 (not profitable) then

WLScore = xRAR × ( 1 + Abs( MaxDrawDownPct ) )


Taking the same example with an APR of -8.0, we obtain a Wealth-Lab Score as follows:

( -8.0 / 0.25 ) * ( 1 + 0.15 ) = -32 * 1.15 = -36.80


**Commission / Cash Interest / Margin Interest / Dividends (Paid or Received)**  
- The total dollar amount of commissions paid for simulated trade executions. 
- The total dollar amount of cash interest earned for simulated trade executions. 
- The total dollar amount of interest paid for margin loans during the backtest period. 
- The total dollar amount of dividends received for simulated trade executions. 


**Positions Count**  
The total number of round-trip trades plus open positions.  For Benchmark, this will equal the number of symbols in the DataSet that was selected for the backtest.

**Avg Profit and Avg Profit %**  
The average profit (loss) per trade less commissions and slippage, in absolute values and as percentage.

**Profit Factor**  
Profit Factor is the system's Gross Profit divided by Gross Loss.  Look for systems that have a 
Profit Factor of 2 or higher.  If your strategy has only winning trades and no losing trades, 
then Profit Factor is Infinity. 

**Payoff Ratio**  
Payoff Ratio is the system's average percentage profit per trade divided by the average percentage loss per trade.  Unless the system has a particularly high Win/Loss ratio, we look for high Payoff Ratios.

**Avg Bars Held**  
The average number of bars held per trade.

**Avg Trades Per Month**  
*Self-descriptive*

**NSF Position Count**  
The number of positions that were not included in the backtest because there was not enough simulated cash to trade their signals.  If you uncheck **Retain NSF Positions** in the 
[Advanced Strategy Settings](AdvancedStrategySettings), this number will always be zero. 

**Max Drawdown**  
Max Drawdown is the largest peak to valley dollar decline in the system's Equity Curve. Wealth-Lab reports Drawdown on a closing price basis.

**Max Drawdown Date**  
Date on which the Max $ Drawdown was realized. 

**Max Drawdown %**  
Max Drawdown % is the largest peak to valley percentage decline in the system's Equity Curve on a closing price basis.

**Max Drawdown % Date**  
Date on which the Max Drawdown % was realized.  The date on which the Max Drawdown % occurred is not necessarily the same as the Max $ Drawdown Date.  This is especially the case for highly-profitable simulations. 

**Recovery Factor**  
Recovery Factor is equal to the absolute value of Net Profit divided by Max Drawdown.  Recovery Factor should typically be larger than 1.  A healthy Recovery Factor, especially one that is greater than that of Benchmark, indicates a strategy's ability to overcome a drawdown.

**% Profitable**  
The percentage of all trades that were marked winners (losers) as defined in the previous paragraph. 

**Avg Profit (Loss)**  
The average profit (loss) per trade less commissions and slippage, absolute value and percentage.

**Winning Trades and Losing Trades Breakdown**  
The performance summary next breaks the trades down into winners and losers (a trade is marked a winner if its return after costs is above zero), and presents the same metrics as the previous section.
